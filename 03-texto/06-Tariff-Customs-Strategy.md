1.2 Justification with the General Interpretative Rules

          The classification follows the General Rules in article 2 of the LIGIE, which reproduce
the Harmonized System rules.

           1. Rule 1 (heading and notes). The product is chicken meat, so it falls in heading
              02.07, "meat and edible offal of the poultry of heading 01.05, fresh, chilled or

              frozen". Chapter 2 Note 1 excludes only products unfit for human consumption,
                                                                                                     4

           insects, guts and animal fats, none of which apply. Chapter 16 Note 1 confirms that
           meat preserved only by the processes of Chapter 2, such as freezing, stays in Chapter

           2. That rules out 1602.32.
         2. Rule 6 (subheadings). Inside 02.07, subheadings are compared at the same level.
           Among the Gallus domesticus subheadings, the product is cut (not whole) and frozen

           (not chilled), so it belongs in 0207.14. Within 0207.14 it is not livers (0207.14.02), so

           it goes to "others", 0207.14.99.

         3. NICO. Mexico's statistical subdivision 05 names exactly this product: "filetes de
           pechuga y pechuga, sus trozos y recortes, sin hueso (deshuesada)". NICO 04 is breast

           with bone, which does not fit.

         4. Rule 5(b) (packing). The cartons and inner bags are the normal packing for this kind

           of good, so they are classified with the meat and not separately.
       Composite good, set or principal function? None applies. The product is a single

material (chicken meat), not a mixture of materials (Rule 3(b)), not a set put up for retail sale,

and not a machine with a principal function. Rule 3 is therefore not needed.

       Risk to watch. If a supplier marinates, salts or injects the breast before shipping, the

product would move to 0210.99 or 1602.32, with different duties and permits. The purchase
contract should state "raw, plain, not injected or seasoned", and the broker should compare the

Brazilian sanitary certificate with the invoice before filing.

                            2. Tariff structure and fiscal optimization

2.1 Applicable duty rate
       The general import duty (IGI) for line 0207.14.99 is 75%, but it is temporarily set at

0% ("Ex.") until December 31, 2026.

           Base rate. The LIGIE tariff lists 75% for this line, the rate that applies to imports

           from countries without a preference, such as Brazil.
                                                                                                    5

           Temporary exemption. Article Primero of the decree of January 6, 2023 sets the

           duty at "Ex." for 0207.14.99 for every good entering under definitive import (DOF,
           2023). The decree of December 31, 2025 removed beef, pork, milk, tilapia and

           sausages from the exemption, kept chicken, and extended it until December 31, 2026

           (DOF, 2025).

           Who benefits. The 0% rate is not limited to registered companies. The Padrón de
           Importadores de Productos de la Canasta Básica gives two extra benefits only:

           proving non-tariff requirements with a simple written statement (Licencia Única

           Universal), and keeping the 0% rate until March 31, 2027 for purchases under

           contracts filed with the SAT by January 8, 2027.
           Which date counts. Under article 56 of the Customs Law, the rate is the one in force

           on the day the vessel anchors at Altamira, not the day of purchase. A container that

           anchors on January 2, 2027 pays 75% if the decree is not renewed.

2.2 Type of duty
       The IGI is an ad valorem duty: a percentage of the customs value. Mexico also uses

specific duties (a fixed amount per unit, such as pesos per kilogram) and mixed duties (both).

With an ad valorem duty, a higher price or a higher freight rate also raises the duty, so the cost of

freight matters twice when the 75% rate applies.

5.2 Trade defense measures
             Antidumping duty: charged when a product is sold to Mexico below its normal price
             in the country of origin and hurts Mexican producers.

             Countervailing duty for subsidies: charged when the exporting government
             subsidizes the product.

             Safeguard: a temporary duty or quota when imports grow so fast that they seriously
             injure national producers, even without unfair practices.
                                                                                                           13

         None applies to chicken from Brazil. No antidumping or countervailing duty on

Brazilian chicken was found in the Secretaría de Economía's publications. The chicken measure
Mexico does apply is an antidumping duty on leg quarters from the United States, first imposed
in 2012 (25.7% for four named exporters and 127.5% for the rest) and kept after a sunset review

in December 2023 (Secretaría de Economía, 2023). It covers a different product and a different
origin. The Mexican poultry industry has asked for a quota on Brazilian boneless breast, so the

measure to watch for 2027 is a quota, not a countervailing duty.

                                 6. Strategic selection of the customs regime
6.1 Selected regime

         Definitive import (importación definitiva), pedimento key A1, defined in article 96 of
the Customs Law as the entry of foreign goods to remain in Mexico for an unlimited time.
6.2 Strategic justification
         What PROBOCA will do with the goods: use them as raw material in its own
production process in Apodaca. The breast is tempered, portioned, injected, frozen again and

packed, and then sold in Mexico to industrial customers, retail chains and under the Dos Familias
brand. The goods stay in Mexico, so definitive import is the regime that matches the business
model.

                           7. Intellectual property protection strategy

       MEMORANDUM
       To: Adrián González Rodríguez, Director of Procurement and Business Intelligence,
PROBOCA
       From: Team, NI2003B Group 504, Tecnológico de Monterrey
       Date: November 3, 2026

       Subject: Trademark protection for the frozen chicken breast import project
       Recommendation: before the first container, confirm in IMPI's database that the
brand on the cartons (Seara) and PROBOCA's own brand for the finished product (Dos
Familias) are registered in Mexico in class 29, and add a trademark clause to the supply
contract.

       1. A brand is only protected in Mexico if it is registered in Mexico. Trademark rights
are territorial. Anyone may use a brand, but the right to its exclusive use is obtained only by
registering it with the Mexican Institute of Industrial Property (Ley Federal de Protección a la
Propiedad Industrial, art. 170). A registration in Brazil gives the supplier no rights here. If the
Seara brand printed on the cartons were not registered in Mexico, a third party could register it

first and challenge the product's sale; if it is registered by someone other than the supplier,
PROBOCA could be importing goods that infringe that owner's rights. The same applies to Dos
Familias: the products PROBOCA makes with the imported breast are only protected if that
brand's registration is in force for meat and poultry. A registration lasts ten years and can be
renewed for equal periods (art. 178).
       2. The institution: the Instituto Mexicano de la Propiedad Industrial (IMPI). IMPI

registers trademarks and resolves infringement cases. Registrations can be checked online in
IMPI's search tool, MARCia, filtering by owner, brand and class 29 (meat, poultry and meat
products), and applications are filed through IMPI's online trademark service. The supply
contract should also require Seara to warrant that it owns or is licensed to use its brand in
Mexico, and to indemnify PROBOCA if a third party makes a claim.
                                                                                                    17

         3. The main commercial benefit: the right to stop others and to keep the brand's
value. With a registered brand, PROBOCA can ask IMPI to order provisional measures,
including withdrawing infringing goods from the market and suspending the free circulation of
infringing goods under any customs regime (art. 344, fr. I and VI). In practice, this means
counterfeit or look-alike products can be stopped at the border before they reach PROBOCA's

customers. It also protects the investment in Dos Familias: a registered brand can be licensed,
used as collateral and valued as an asset, while an unregistered one can be lost to whoever files
first.
         Next step: the legal team runs the MARCia search for Seara and Dos Familias in class 29
and attaches the results to the supplier file before the purchase order.
