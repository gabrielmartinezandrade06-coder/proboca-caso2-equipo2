Import Manual — PROBOCA (Part 1)
2026-09-24 · Prepared by Gabriel Martínez

1. Introduction

This manual is a strategic guide for improving the import operations of PROBOCA, a Mexican meat
processor, by identifying where to source turkey, pork and boneless chicken, at what landed cost, and
under what regulatory and sanitary risk. This first document lays the foundation; it will be extended with
each module of the Challenge.

Scope. Our team's case is Case 2: boneless skinless chicken (breast and leg meat) from Brazil,
benchmarked against US chicken under USMCA. Turkey and pork appear as context for PROBOCA's
overall import strategy. Candidate origins: Brazil, the United States, Canada and selected European
countries. Buyer: PROBOCA, which contracts roughly 70% of its volume annually (October) and buys the
remaining ~30% on the spot market.

General objective

Provide a logistics, tariff and supplier-country strategy that lowers PROBOCA's landed cost and reduces its
exposure to regulatory shocks, without sacrificing product quality or supply continuity.

Specific objectives

    1. Describe PROBOCA and the business situation that makes a new import strategy necessary.

    2. Diagnose PROBOCA's import operations through a SWOT analysis.

    3. Select potential supplier countries per product, using hard data and structured frameworks (PESTLE
       and a weighted scoring matrix).

    4. Identify risks and opportunities tied to Mexico's 2026 import quotas, tariffs and sanitary requirements.

    5. Recommend strategies for customs and regulatory compliance, to be developed in later modules.

and based in Apodaca, Nuevo León, about 120 miles south of Laredo, Texas (PROBOCA). The

Business Intelligence (he introduced himself in class as Chief Procurement Officer, so this manual calls him
"the CPO"). He presented the Challenge at the welcome session on 23 September 2026.

3. Description of the Situation

PROBOCA's challenge is to keep buying turkey, pork and chicken at a competitive landed cost
while the rules that made its current sourcing cheap are changing in 2026, and while its main turkey
origin, the United States, is supply-constrained. Four forces converge:

    1. A new tariff-rate quota on non-FTA pork and beef. Since 6 January 2026 Mexico allows only
       51,000 t of pork and 70,000 t of beef to enter duty-free from countries without a trade agreement
       (Brazil is the main target); the quota ends on 31 December 2026 (Porcicultura, DOF, pork
         agreement). The CPO reported that the 2026 volume was exhausted by August and that the 2027
         allocation will be published around 1 December. Quota certificates cost roughly US$1/kg for pork and
         MX$6.50/kg for beef (CPO statement, to be validated against the auction results).

    2. Chicken is next in line. Chicken from non-FTA countries is still tariff-free under the anti-inflation
       decree, which runs to 31 December 2026 (USDA FAS). Mexico's poultry association is asking the
       government to replace the exemption with a quota for Brazilian boneless breast (Mexico Business
       News, 25 Aug 2026). The CPO expects a quota next year.

    3. US turkey supply is tight and US access is under review. US turkey production fell to about 195
       million birds in 2025, the lowest in four decades, and the USDA forecast a 40% price increase for
       frozen hen turkey (American Farm Bureau, Oct 2025). On 1 July 2026 the United States declined to
       renew the USMCA, which now faces annual reviews; the agreement stays in force (White & Case).

    4. Sanitary shocks. Poland, Europe's largest poultry exporter, recorded 143 avian influenza outbreaks
       by May 2026; Spain's African swine fever in wild boars led Mexico to suspend Spanish pork in
       December 2025 (The Pig Site); Mexico reopened Brazilian chicken in June 2025 except for Rio
       Grande do Sul.

The United States supplies about 85% of Mexico's turkey imports and PROBOCA has no European turkey
supplier today (CPO statement). The question for this manual is where else PROBOCA can source,
and under which tariff and sanitary conditions, before the 2027 contracts are signed in October.

Our case. PROBOCA's kick-off deck frames the Challenge as designing a real import operation, from
supplier prospecting to a signed supply contract, and defending it before a purchasing committee. Our team
works Case 2, boneless chicken from Brazil under sanitary and tariff uncertainty, and must plan for two
2027 scenarios because "nobody knows the 2027 rules yet." Brazil is the world's largest chicken exporter
and structurally cheaper than the US; the open question is reliability.
