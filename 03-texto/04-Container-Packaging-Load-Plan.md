    Container, Packaging and Load Plan: Frozen Boneless Chicken Breast from Brazil to
                                    Apodaca (PROBOCA, Case 2)
           This document sets the minimum physical requirements for PROBOCA's import of
frozen boneless skinless chicken breast from Brazil: the container, the packing and packaging,
and how the cargo is arranged inside each means of transport. It builds on the route selected in
our logistics route design: Seara's plant in Lapa, Paraná (SIF 530), 151 km by truck to the port of
Paranaguá, a reefer container on the Maersk UHA-GULFEX service to Altamira (30 days),
customs and sanitary clearance at Altamira, and 492 km by truck to PROBOCA's plant in
Apodaca, Nuevo León (Valdez Gómez et al., 2026c). Aurora Coop, shipping from Itajaí on the
same vessel loop, is the second source, and the same specification applies to it.
           The product is frozen at −18 °C, dense and sensitive to temperature and contamination.
Those three facts drive every choice that follows: the cargo needs a refrigerated container with its
own cooling unit, packaging that keeps its strength in cold and humid conditions, and a load plan
that fills the container to its weight limit while leaving the air channels the refrigeration needs.
           Facts and estimates. Container dimensions, legal limits and official tariffs come from
primary sources and are cited. Carton dimensions, carton weight and some costs are our proposal
or estimates; they are marked as such and will be confirmed with the suppliers' answers to the

request for quotation (RFQ).

             Condition: pre-trip inspection of the refrigeration unit before release to the plant, a
             clean and odor-free interior, and a working temperature recorder.

             Power on the road: clip-on generator on both truck legs (Lapa to Paranaguá and
             Altamira to Apodaca).

             Monitoring: an independent temperature logger placed by the shipper, plus the
             carrier's remote reefer monitoring where available.

                              3. Conclusions and Recommendation
       PROBOCA should import in full 40-ft high-cube reefer containers (FCL) moved as

full truckloads (TL), each carrying 20 heat-treated pallets of 65 cartons of 20 kg: 1,300
cartons and 26.0 t net. The evidence:

            The 40-ft high-cube reefer is the only efficient box. It carries 26 t, against 10.8 t in
            a 20-ft reefer, and costs 41.9 US cents of logistics per kilogram, against 71–84 cents
            in the 20-ft option. The 45-ft pallet-wide box adds volume that this dense cargo

            cannot use.
            Weight is the binding limit, not space. The load uses 89% of the payload and 76%
            of the usable volume; the free space is the air the refrigeration unit needs. A shared

            container would charge this cargo by volume (53.5 revenue tons for 27.6 t), which
            confirms FCL.

            The building block works without waste. The 600 × 400 mm carton covers the
            Brazilian PBR pallet completely, and the 20 pallets cover 90% of the floor in two
            lanes.

            Pallets are worth their cost. They add US$181 per container, less than one cent per
            kilogram, do not reduce the product per container, and make unloading at the
            Altamira inspection and in Apodaca fast, which protects the cold chain.

            The truck in Mexico must have 6 axles. Truck plus container weighs about 46.8 t:
            within the 54.0 t of a T3-S3, but over the 46.5 t of a T3-S2.

       To confirm before the first order: the supplier's real carton size, net weight and cartons
per pallet; PROBOCA's monthly volume; whether PROBOCA's dock has push-pull forklifts, in
which case slip-sheets can replace the wood pallets; and real quotes for ocean freight and

trucking.

                                Calculations
Cartons per pallet layer: pallet face 1.20 × 1.00 m = 1.20 m²; carton face 0.60 ×
0.40 m = 0.24 m²; 1.20 ÷ 0.24 = 5 cartons (3 turned across the 1.20 m side plus 2
lengthwise).
Layers per pallet: usable height 2.394 m minus pallet 0.15 m = 2.244 m; 2.244 ÷
0.16 = 14.0, so 14 layers would reach 2.39 m, only 4 mm under the load line. We use

13 layers (2.23 m) to keep a 16-cm margin.
Cartons and weight per pallet: 5 × 13 = 65 cartons × 20 kg = 1,300 kg net.
Pallets per container: lane A, pallets 1.00 m long: 11.584 ÷ 1.00 = 11; lane B, pallets
1.20 m long: 11.584 ÷ 1.20 = 9; total 20 pallets, 1,300 cartons, 26.0 t net.
Use of the container: carton volume 1,300 × 0.0384 m³ = 49.9 m³, 76% of 65.3 m³;

26.0 t of 29.26 t payload = 89%; floor 20 × 1.20 m² = 24.0 m² of 26.57 m² = 90%.
Gross weights: 26,000 kg net + 1,300 × 0.9 kg of packaging + 20 × 22 kg of pallets =
27,610 kg; plus 4,740 kg of tare = 32,350 kg verified gross mass.
Mexican road check: 32.35 t + tractor 9.0 t + chassis 4.5 t + generator 0.95 t = 46.8 t
(vehicle weights are our estimates).

20-ft reefer: palletized, 5 + 4 = 9 pallets of 12 layers (60 cartons) = 540 cartons =
10.8 t; floor-loaded, 9 rows × 5 × 13 layers = 585 cartons = 11.7 t.
Carton strength: the bottom carton carries 12 cartons × about 20.9 kg = 251 kg; with
a safety factor of 4 for cold and humidity, the box compression test should be at least
1,000 kgf.

Volumetric weights: 53.5 m³ × 1,000 kg = 53.5 revenue tons (sea); × 333 kg =
17,816 kg (road, illustrative); × 167 kg = 8,935 kg (air).
