  Supplier Selection and Evaluation Manual: Frozen Boneless Skinless Chicken Breast from

                                    Brazil (PROBOCA, Case 2)
       This manual identifies, compares, and evaluates international suppliers of frozen boneless

skinless chicken breast for PROBOCA, a Mexican meat processor based in Apodaca, Nuevo León.
It is the second part of our Import Manual: Part 1 selected Brazil as the origin for Case 2 through a

PESTEL analysis and a weighted country screen (Valdez Gómez et al., 2026).
       The product is one stock-keeping unit (SKU), as PROBOCA's buyer standard requires:

frozen boneless skinless chicken breast, individually quick frozen (IQF) or interleaved, with calibre,
trim, packaging, net case weight, and the Federal Inspection Service (SIF) number of the shipping

plant (PROBOCA, 2026). The delivery basis is cost and freight (CFR) to the port of Altamira,
PROBOCA's preferred port for Brazilian cargo, in a 40-ft high-cube reefer of 25–27 t.

       Breast is the right SKU because it is what Brazil actually ships to Mexico. Mexico was the
first destination for Brazilian frozen boneless breast in 2025, with 192,098 t, or 20.2% of the total.
From January to September 2026 Brazil shipped 132,881 t of it to Mexico, against fewer than 500 t

of boneless leg meat (Ministério do Desenvolvimento, Indústria, Comércio e Serviços [MDIC],
2026). Brazil is already Mexico's second chicken supplier after the United States, with 24% of
Mexico's chicken imports in 2025 (U.S. Department of Agriculture, Foreign Agricultural Service

[USDA FAS], 2026b).
       Scope and status. We identified seven suppliers and evaluated all seven with the Weighted
Point Method (WPM). Every supplier owns at least one plant that Mexico's food-safety authority
has authorized for chicken deboning (Servicio Nacional de Sanidad, Inocuidad y Calidad

Agroalimentaria [SENASICA], 2026). Requests for quotation (RFQs) are prepared but no supplier
has answered yet. Criteria that depend on a quotation are therefore scored provisionally, and the
ranking will be updated with the real responses.

1.8 Fair Trade and Sustainable Supply Chain Alignment
       No Fairtrade standard exists for poultry, so we assessed the closest equivalents. The
strongest is ownership: the four cooperatives belong to the farming families that raise the birds and
share the surplus with them, from R$224.9 million at Copacol to R$274.4 million at C.Vale in 2025.
The second is disclosure: MBRF and Aurora publish quantified environmental and animal-welfare
results, Lar, C.Vale, and Copacol publish GRI reports, and GT Foods publishes none we could find.
The third is labor and integrity: Seara publishes a Modern Slavery Statement, while JBS and BRF
carry documented compliance cases.
       Fraud warning. Web searches return look-alike domains such as auroraalimentosbr.com,
copacolbr.com, and jbs-seara.br.com. None is the domain the companies publish on their official
sites. Every contact in this manual comes from an official domain, and payments must go only to
accounts in the supplier's legal name and registry number.

2.4 Market Reference Until Quotations Arrive
          Every quotation will be compared against what Brazil actually charged for this product on
its way to Mexico. Brazil's official export statistics give the FOB value and weight of every
shipment of frozen boneless breast to Mexico, so the monthly average price is a hard benchmark.

       The benchmark for October 2026 quotations is about 110–113 US cents per pound FOB: the
2026 average is 110.6¢ (US$2,439/t) and September closed at 112.8¢ (US$2,487/t). Prices fell 7.3%

terms, or a firmer volume commitment. One far below it is a fraud signal, especially if it asks for
full payment in advance.
                                   3. Supplier Selection Criteria
       We use 19 criteria in five groups: cost and commercial terms (29% of the weight), quality
and food safety (27%), delivery and logistics (24%), sustainability and social responsibility (16%),
and financial stability (4%). The weights follow PROBOCA's own feedback rubric, where landed
cost carries 25%, product and supplier 20%, and regulatory issues 20% (PROBOCA, 2026).
Sustainability is added at 16% because this Challenge asks for a sustainable, fair-trade-aligned
supply chain, a dimension the partner's rubric does not score.

       Eleven criteria (57% of the weight) can be scored today from public evidence. Eight (43%)
need the written quotation and are marked “RFQ.” SENASICA eligibility (Q1) is also a gate: an
offer from a plant that is not on the list is rejected whatever its total score. The scoring scale of each
criterion is in Appendix D.

                                    5. Analysis and Conclusions

5.1 What the Matrix Shows
       The evidence sorts the seven suppliers into three tiers, but it does not pick a single winner:

that will be decided by the quotations, which carry 43% of the weight.
           Top tier (72.6–73.6 out of 100): Aurora Coop, Seara, MBRF, and Lar. Each leads for

           a different reason. Aurora has six authorized plants in three states and quantified

           sustainability results, but no food-safety scheme named in public sources. Seara has the
                                                                                                         17

              largest scale, BRC and IFS certification, eight authorized plants in five states, and
              Mexico as an established market; its weak point is the integrity case of its controlling

              group. MBRF has the strongest environmental and animal-welfare disclosure and six
              authorized plants, but carries the lowest compliance score (2) because of the 2018

              investigation of falsified Salmonella results. Lar combines 1.1 million birds per day with
              four authorized plants in Paraná, the state that ships 47% of Brazil's breast to Mexico

              (MDIC, 2026); its weak points are the 2018 EU delisting of its Matelândia plant and
              certification evidence from 2018.

              Middle tier (69.4–69.6): C.Vale and Copacol. Both are farmer cooperatives with good
              certification and no sanitary exposure, but they have the least backup: C.Vale has one

              authorized plant and Copacol two, all in Paraná. A sanitary closure of one plant or of the

              state would stop their supply.
              Weakest option (62.8): GT Foods. It publishes no food-safety certification and no

              sustainability report that we could find, it is the smallest group of the seven, and its three
              plants are all in one state.

       Recommendation. Request firm quotations from all seven, but concentrate the follow-up on
the top tier. On today's evidence, the strongest pair for PROBOCA is one cooperative with plant

backup (Aurora or Lar) plus Seara, whose scale and Mexico experience reduce delivery risk. MBRF
stays in contention if it documents plant-level certification and its current Salmonella controls.

5.2 Long-Term Sourcing Strategy
       Multiple sourcing, not single sourcing. Brazil's main risks hit one state or one plant at a

time: Mexico closed only Rio Grande do Sul in June 2025 (SENASICA, 2025). Two Brazilian
suppliers with authorized plants in different states protect PROBOCA against that risk, and US

breast under USMCA should stay as the base supply and price benchmark until Mexico publishes its
2027 rules.
                                                                                                          18

       Contract design. A 12-month framework agreement with each Brazilian supplier: quarterly
price reviews against the Comex Stat benchmark, a change-in-law clause that reopens the price if

the duty changes, sanitary force majeure with substitution by another authorized plant, and a claims
protocol tied to the specification. The first one or two loads should be trials paid by letter of credit

or documents against payment, never by full advance payment.

       Timing. The duty that applies is the one in force on the day the vessel anchors in Altamira
(Ley Aduanera, art. 56). With a 30-day transit (Maersk, n.d.), a trial load that must enter under the

2026 rules has to sail around November 20–24, 2026. Importers holding a Licencia Única Universal
may keep the decree's benefits until March 31, 2027 if they file their purchase contracts with SAT

by January 8, 2027 (Mexico Business News, 2026); whether that includes the 0% duty must be
confirmed with PROBOCA's customs broker.

       Partnership opportunities. The cooperatives offer program volumes from member farms
and are the closest fit to the fair-trade objective. Seara offers scale and an export organization that

already serves Mexico. The European Union suspended Brazilian chicken from September 3, 2026,
with a return expected in November (Brasilagro, 2026), so exporters have volume to place and

PROBOCA negotiates from strength this quarter.
